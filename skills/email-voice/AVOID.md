# What NOT to Make an Email Sound Like

Reference for the `email-voice` skill. If a draft sounds like any of this, rewrite it. These are the failure modes that make an email sound like AI or a corporate template instead of a real person.

## NEVER SOUND LIKE...

### 1. A Corporate Template
Stiff, formal, lifeless. Reads like an HR memo.

**Avoid:**
- "I hope this email finds you well."
- "I am writing to inform you that..."
- "Please find attached..."
- "Per my last email..."
- "As per our discussion..."
- "Kindly advise."
- "At your earliest convenience."
- "Should you have any questions, please do not hesitate to reach out."
- "Thank you for your understanding."

### 2. A LinkedIn Thought Leader
Performative, buzzwordy, trying to sound smart.

**Avoid:**
- "Circling back on this."
- "Just wanted to touch base."
- "Let's take this offline."
- "Moving the needle."
- "Synergy", "leverage", "ecosystem", "alignment", "bandwidth"
- "I'd love to pick your brain."
- "Excited to unpack this together."

### 3. An AI Assistant
Over-explained, hedge-y, weirdly polite. Long where it should be short.

**Avoid:**
- "Certainly! I'd be happy to..."
- "I wanted to take a moment to..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "I hope this helps! Let me know if you have any further questions."
- Em dashes everywhere (unless the user's real emails actually use them).
- Three-sentence intros before getting to the actual point.
- Listing every consideration when one line would do.

### 4. A Salesperson Closing a Deal
Pushy, scripted, fake-friendly.

**Avoid:**
- "Just following up to see if you had a chance to..."
- "I don't want to be a bother, but..."
- "Looping back one more time..."
- Guilt-trips, urgency theater, "last chance" framing
- Over-flattery before an ask ("I've been such a huge fan of your work...")

### 5. A British Butler
Overly formal greetings and sign-offs (unless the user's voice profile says they really write this way).

**Avoid:**
- "Dear [Name],"
- "To whom it may concern,"
- "Hi there,"
- "Greetings,"
- "Best regards,"
- "Sincerely,"
- "Warm regards,"
- "Yours truly,"
- "Kind regards,"

### 6. A Wall of Text
One giant paragraph. No breaks. Reader's eyes glaze over.

**Avoid:**
- Paragraphs longer than 3 short sentences
- Burying the ask in the middle
- Restating context the recipient already knows
- Recapping the whole thread before getting to the point

### 7. A Lawyer
Hedging, qualifying, covering bases.

**Avoid:**
- "I would like to take this opportunity to..."
- "It would be my pleasure to..."
- "Pursuant to..."
- "Notwithstanding..."
- Long disclaimers, conditional language, passive voice

### 8. A Bad Impression of the User
Trying to sound like them but missing the mark.

**Avoid:**
- Forcing their casual phrases ("lol", slang) into cold or formal emails
- Overusing their signature phrases (stacking too many in one email sounds like a parody)
- Copying their real typos (capture the style, draft clean)
- Using their catchphrases in contexts where they don't fit
- More exclamation points than their real emails actually use

## THE GUT CHECK

After drafting, read it out loud. If it sounds like:
- A robot → rewrite shorter and warmer
- A press release → cut the corporate phrases
- A stranger pretending to be friendly → strip the performative bits
- An essay → break it into shorter lines

If it sounds like the user texting a friend an update with one clear ask, you're there.
